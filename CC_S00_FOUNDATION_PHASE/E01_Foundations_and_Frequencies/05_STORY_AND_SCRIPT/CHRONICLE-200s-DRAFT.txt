TITLE: Chronicles of Cupid Black — The Signal Before the Story

[NARRATOR]

It began like many school mysteries do: with a missing answer, a strange rumour, and somebody insisting that nothing unusual had happened.

Cupid Enka was at Blue Crown, trying to finish his studies, keep his relationships from becoming a full-time occupation, and understand why his own brain sometimes seemed to be several pages ahead of him.

Some days, answers arrived before he remembered studying them.

Other days, a familiar subject became a blank wall.

Then came the records.

A module was missing.

The administration said he had not completed it.

Enka disagreed.

And somewhere between the missing paper and the missing explanation, another pattern appeared.

A hum.

A strange signal.

Towers that were supposedly for communications.

Frequencies that did not behave like ordinary school equipment.

So Enka did what any sensible electronics enthusiast would do.

He built an RF scanner.

Which, naturally, was the moment sensible behaviour left the building.

[LIGHT COMEDIC BEAT]

He followed the signal, watched the meter climb, took one more step backward—

BANG.

Door.

Pride: injured.

RF scanner: still working.

Enka: considerably less impressed with science.

But the signal was real.

At least, something was real.

He began keeping records.

Dreams.

Missing time.

Sudden flashes of information.

Moments when a place felt familiar before he had ever been there.

And then came the question that would follow him for years:

Was he discovering the experiment—

or was he the experiment?

[PAUSE]

The old records tell us that Enka was being observed.

They tell us that his response to the technology was unusual.

They tell us that other students were affected in different ways.

But they do not yet tell us everything.

Because sometimes the most important part of a story is the part the archive does not understand.

One night, Enka looked toward the signal and felt the world shift.

For a fraction of a second, he seemed to occupy two places at once.

One was where he stood.

The other was somewhere else.

He blinked.

The second place disappeared.

He stared into the darkness.

"I don't even know what is real anymore."

Years later, another name would begin to follow him.

Cupid Black.

But that name belongs to a later chapter.

For now, we have a student, a signal, a mystery—

and an archive that may have recorded the beginning of something much bigger.

The signal had not disappeared.

It had only learned to wait.

[END]
