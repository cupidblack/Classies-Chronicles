Unified Production & Moderation Playbook 

(For a 20–30 minute Google Meet → 4-minute cinematic edit) 

  

Purpose 

- Convert a 20–30 minute cross-country conversation (Canada, UK, South Africa, Ghana) into a tight ~4-minute episode that introduces tension, contrast, and real-world examples following a calm, structured Part 1. 

- Harvest 6–10 headline-ready soundbites, 2–3 concrete examples, a moment of disagreement, a brief ethical/spiritual reflection (optional), and an unresolved closing question to drive a series arc.

High-level creative strategy 

- Tone: Global, intelligent, slightly tense, curious (not preachy). 

- Emotional arc for the final 4-minute cut: Shock → Tension → Real-world examples → Unease → Reflection. 

- Goal: Leave audience slightly unsettled and curious for Part 3 (adaptation/solutions). 

  

1. Roles & Energy Design (Before the Meet) 

- Assign each panelist an informal perspective so the discussion avoids polite unanimity: 

  - The Optimist: AI creates more jobs than it destroys. 

  - The Skeptic: AI replaces most mid-level roles. 

  - The Practitioner: Already uses AI in real work. 

  - The Worrier: Concerned about youth and informal sector impacts. 

- Send 3 sharp questions in advance (so answers are prepared and editable): 

  1. What job category disappears first in your country because of AI? 

  2. What skill becomes non-negotiable in the next 5 years? 

  3. If advising a 19-year-old today, what would you tell them? 

  

2. Participant Prep Checklist (Send by WhatsApp / Email) 

- Prepare and optionally send before the call: 

  1. One sentence headline (5–15s): “Which job/task disappears first in your country because of AI?” 

  2. One concrete example (30–60s): A real case where AI compressed or replaced work. 

  3. One practical action (15–30s): What listeners should do tomorrow. 

  4. Optional vertical phone clip (10–20s) for promos. 

- Technical checklist: 

  - Join 10–15 minutes early. 

  - Use headphones. 

  - Camera at eye level, face a light source. 

  - Stable internet, quiet room, neutral background.

3. Timeboxed Agenda (20–30 min raw; guides the recording) 

- 0:00–0:40 — Host opener + verbal consent on camera 

- 0:40–2:00 — Headline round: each guest delivers 20–25s bold prediction 

- 2:00–7:00 — Concrete examples: 60–90s per guest (push for specificity) 

- 7:00–13:00 — Cross-country comparison & mild friction (call-outs and pushes) 

- 13:00–18:00 — Human skills & adaptation (30–45s each) 

- 18:00–22:00 — Genesis ethical pivot (optional, 30–60s total) 

- 22:00–24:00 — Practical action round (15–20s each) 

- 24:00–30:00 — Buffer, re-takes, optional portrait clips, extras 

  

4. Live Moderation: Host Role & Script (Verbatim + Tips) 

Opening / Consent: 

  “Hello — I’m Elliot. Thank you for joining. We’re recording for Classies Chronicles. When I call your name, please say ‘I consent’ on camera.” 

Headline Prompt: 

  “In one sentence: which job or task will disappear first in your country because of AI?” 

Example Prompt: 

  “Give one concrete example you’ve seen — who, what changed, and the effect?” 

Tension / Challenge Prompts: 

  - “You said AI won’t replace creative roles. Isn’t that already happening?” 

  - “If entry-level roles disappear, where will young people begin?” 

Genesis Pivot (Optional & Non-Dogmatic): 

  “I’ll introduce a reflective lens. Genesis 1:28 speaks about stewardship and Genesis 6:3 cautions human limitation. Do you see a spiritual or ethical dimension to AI — or would you prefer to pass? Feel free to pass.” 

Closing: 

  “Thank you — please confirm in chat that we may use your edited contribution. Optional: send a 10–20s promo clip after the call.” 

  

Moderation energy rules: 

- Interrupt gently if someone goes long: “Give me the 15-second version.” 

- Call people by name to increase tension and focus. 

- Use short follow-ups: “Shorten that.” “Do you disagree?” “One sentence if possible.” 

- Ask for specifics: company name, role, exact effect — vague statements edit poorly. 

  

5. Structure for the Raw Call (For Editors / Live Producer) 

Use a 4-block approach (for harvesting soundbites): 

- Block 1 (3–4 min): Shock Question — quick, punchy predictions. 

- Block 2 (5–6 min): Real Examples — push for concrete details. 

- Block 3 (5–6 min): Disagreement — intentionally pick a bold claim and challenge it. 

- Block 4 (3–4 min): Human Question — dignity, identity, what happens to middle management? 

  

6. Recording Strategy & Tools (Redundancy = safety) 

Preferred: 

- Riverside.fm (primary): separate local tracks, highest quality. 

Backup stack (if Riverside not used): 

- Primary: Google Meet native recording (host) → Drive 

- Backup: OBS Studio screen + audio local capture 

- Transcription: Sider or Otter.ai (or Descript for post-edit) 

- Optional extras: Ask participants to record local phone voice memos of their prepared lines. 

Best practices: 

- Record at 1080p; capture system audio + mic; record separate tracks where possible. 

- Never rely on one recording source. 

  

7. Google Meet View & Camera Guidance 

- Default: Tiled (shows all participants; feels democratic). 

- Spotlight briefly for strong lines (10–20s) to create cinematic emphasis. 

- Avoid: Auto (unpredictable) and Sidebar-only. 

- Camera checklist for participants: 

  - Eye-level camera, face a light source, headphones, quiet room, neutral background, solid-colored clothing. 

  

8. Visual & Production Techniques (Post) 

- Opening montage: each participant says a bold sentence (4 × 5–7s). 

- Use lower-thirds: Name + City/Country (“Accra, Ghana”). 

- Cut to reaction faces for controversial statements. 

- Use subtle zoom-ins on emphasis. 

- Add subtitles for bold quotes and social verticals. 

- Light background instrumental bed (very subtle) — optional. 

- Fast cuts: no one speaks longer than 20–25s without a cut in the final edit. 

  

9. Editing Strategy (20–30 min → 4:00) 

Macro editing arc (timings for 4:00 final): 

- 0:00–0:20 — Shock/Hook montage (4 strong lines), lower-thirds 

- 0:20–1:20 — Cross-country predictions & concrete examples 

- 1:20–2:30 — Real-world examples & specifics 

- 2:30–3:20 — Disagreement / tension moment 

- 3:20–3:50 — Short philosophical/spiritual reflection (Genesis pivot) — optional 

- 3:50–4:00 — End on unresolved question and a beat of silence 

Editing rules: 

- Cut ruthlessly: remove long intros, “can you hear me?”, repeated agreement, hesitation. 

- Keep bold statements, emotional reactions, contradictions, and clear predictions. 

- Pacing: aim for cuts every 6–12 seconds in the final cut; no >25s continuous speaking. 

- Audio: normalize, mild compression, gentle EQ; subtitles for accessibility. 

- Create vertical/shorts cuts for promos. 

  

10. Spiritual / Genesis Pivot — Wording & Caution 

- Do not assert theological claims. Frame the pivot as reflective: 

  Suggested phrasing for live use: 

  “Let me introduce a reflective lens. Genesis 1:28 speaks of humans ‘subduing the earth,’ which some read as stewardship; Genesis 6:3 reminds us of human limitation. Do you see a moral or spiritual dimension to AI — or prefer to pass? Please feel free to pass.” 

- Keep this segment optional, brief (30–60s raw; 20–30s in final cut), and non-dogmatic. 

- Avoid conspiratorial language or claims that AI originates from a spiritual realm. 

  

11. Legal & Administrative (Consent, Payments, Release) 

- Two-layer consent: Written WhatsApp confirmation + verbal on-camera consent at start. 

  - WhatsApp consent template: 

    “I [Full Name] consent to being recorded and for my edited appearance to be published as part of Classies Chronicles.” 

- Recommended: simple Google Form as release (name, email, country, consent checkbox, typed name). 

- Compensation options (choose & confirm before recording): 

  - Honorarium (small fixed token after publication) — recommended. 

  - Exposure/credit (only if agreed explicitly). 

  - Avoid revenue share without legal documentation. 

- Keep all consent screenshots and form responses stored with the project files.

13. Moderator One-Page Cheat Sheet (Keep beside your screen) 

- Opening (on-camera): “I’m Elliot. When I call your name, say ‘I consent.’ Quick reminder: keep answers tight — 45–60s max; short headlines are gold.” 

- First prompt (headline): “In one sentence — what job disappears first in your country because of AI?” 

- Example prompt: “Give one concrete example — what role, what changed, which company/sector?” 

- Tension prompt (pick a claim): “You said X — isn’t that already happening? Who disagrees?” 

- Genesis bridge: “Is there an ethical/spiritual question here? Feel free to pass.” 

- Close: “Thanks. Please confirm in chat you consent to this edited clip. Can you send a 10–20s promo clip?” 

- Time cues: move everyone along after 60s for keynote answers; after 90s cut and move forward.
